Call for Papers

The Cloud-Edge Continuum is a new hyper-distributed computing paradigm that aims at seamlessly interconnecting and abstracting the computing power and storage capacity of the Cloud with the data processing capabilities of edge devices, IoT devices, and sensors. Intelligently balancing tasks in a continuum enables faster response times, lower latency, and more efficient use of network bandwidth. However, efficiently managing extremely heterogeneous Cloud and Edge resources is a pressing concern in real-world large-scale implementations. Such heterogeneity refers to differences in network capabilities, processing power, software stack, APIs, and security protocols across the Cloud-Edge Continuum infrastructure. This raises significant challenges for the network community to develop new and efficient protocols and communication services. Given the potential benefits of the Cloud-Edge Continuum, it is important to explore the current state-of-the-art research and identify the challenges and opportunities in this area.

The Cloud-Edge Continuum (CEC) workshop aims to bring together researchers and practitioners from academia and industry to discuss the latest research, trends, and challenges in ecosystems and environments based on Cloud-Edge Continuum paradigm. The primary goal of this workshop is to foster collaboration and exchange of ideas among researchers and stakeholders. The workshop can provide a platform for participants to share their experiences, best practices, case studies, and to identify emerging research areas and potential solutions to existing challenges. By bringing together experts from different domains, the CEC workshop also aims to provide insights into how Cloud-Edge Continuum can be applied in various fields, such as healthcare, transportation, smart cities, and manufacturing.

Topics of Interest

We welcome submissions of original research, work-in-progress, and proof of concepts describing original ideas and presenting new directions. We invite submissions covering the aspects around the design, development, and evaluation of architectures, technologies, and applications for the Cloud-Edge Continuum, including but not limited to:

  • Network optimization and interoperable protocols for the Cloud-Edge Continuum
  • Novel system architectures and hardware designs in the Cloud-Edge Continuum
  • Network security, data privacy, confidence, and trust in the Cloud-Edge Continuum
  • Serverless services and models for the Cloud-Edge Continuum
  • Mobile computing and distributed autonomy in the Cloud-Edge Continuum
  • Federated learning, and swarm intelligence in the Cloud-Edge Continuum
  • ML, LLM, and GenAI based requirement gathering and resource allocation in Cloud-Edge Continuum
  • QoS provisioning, QoE, and SLA management in the Cloud-Edge Continuum
  • Sustainable, energy and carbon efficient connectivity in the Cloud-Edge Continuum
  • Next generation smart use-cases enabled by the Cloud-Edge Continuum

Tomas Cerny earned his PhD from the Czech Technical University in Prague. He has held professorships in Prague and at Baylor University and is currently a faculty member at the University of Arizona at the School of Electrical, Computing and Software Engineering. Nariman Mani, PhD, P.Eng.

Dr. Nariman Mani is Chief Technology Officer and Director of Research and Development at NutroSal Inc. His research focuses on dependable AI-enabled software, cloud-native and microservice architectures, software assurance, and privacy-preserving orchestration. He brings more than 18 years of experience spanning software engineering, systems architecture, quality assurance, and technology leadership. His technical work spans cloud-native architectures, software testing, performance engineering, applied AI, and privacy-preserving digital platforms. Dr. Mani holds a PhD in Systems and Computer Engineering from Carleton University and is a Professional Engineer in Ontario, Canada. Alongside his industry work, he contributes to research at the University of Arizona, connecting practical engineering challenges with research in software architecture and dependable AI-enabled systems.

0930–1030 — Session 1: Keynote Speech

Session Chair: Dr. Ahmed Khalid
Speaker: Dr. Tomas Cerny (University of Arizona)
Title: Cloud-Native Change Impact Analysis: Understanding What Will Break Before It Breaks
Abstract: Cloud-native systems enable unprecedented speed of software evolution, but their distributed, heterogeneous, and continuously changing nature makes it increasingly difficult to understand the consequences of change. This keynote explores change impact analysis for cloud-native systems, combining software architecture, static analysis, dependency analysis, and repository mining to identify how changes propagate across services, APIs, data, configuration, and infrastructure. It discusses how technical debt and architectural erosion amplify change impact, share insights from our research on evolving cloud-native systems, and explore how AI can enable more intelligent and trustworthy impact analysis. The talk will highlight a fundamental challenge for modern software engineering: how can we change cloud-native systems rapidly while understanding what those changes may break?

1400–1500 — Session 3: Keynote Speech

Session Chair: Dr. Ahmed Khalid
Speaker: Dr. Nariman Mani, CTO and Director of R&D, NutroSal Inc.
Title: AI in Production: Engineering Secure and Resilient Systems Across the Cloud–Edge Continuum
Abstract: Deploying AI across devices, edge gateways, and cloud services raises a practical question: how do we keep systems useful, secure, and accountable when connectivity is intermittent, resources are constrained, and components fail? From an industrial perspective, production readiness requires more than model accuracy or a successful deployment. It requires explicit decisions about data handling, access permissions, failure detection, and recovery. This industrial keynote examines the engineering decisions behind operating AI-enabled systems across the cloud–edge continuum. It considers which responsibilities should remain local, which require cloud coordination, and which actions should be restricted or deferred when the system cannot verify its data, permissions, or operating conditions. The talk uses architecture scenarios to examine disconnected operation, stale policies, partial service failures, and inconsistent software or model versions. Rather than presenting a single framework as a universal solution, the keynote offers a decision-oriented approach to production readiness and asks: how can we distribute intelligence without losing the ability to detect failure, constrain actions, and restore service?
